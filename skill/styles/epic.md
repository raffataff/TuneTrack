## producer
- 130-170 bpm, minor or Phrygian. A 60-120 s loop: A drives, B lifts (a new layer, a key up a step, or the theme in
  the brass), the loop's last bars build back into A.
- The layers: a string ostinato, low brass and choir for weight, taiko and toms, a synth bass and pulse for the
  hybrid edge. Each section adds or removes a layer.
- Boss cues twist the main theme (minor, augmented, in the low brass) rather than inventing a new one.

## composer
- The engine is an ostinato in 16ths (cellos or violas, or a synth pulse) on a two-chord cell: i-bVI, i-bVII,
  i-bII for menace. It carries the tempo; everything else can be long.
- The theme in long, heroic notes over it: horns in unison, then trombones, then the whole brass at the peak.
  Short, strong motifs (three to five notes) work better than long melodies at this speed.
- Harmony: i-bVI-bVII-i, i-bVI-III-bVII, a bVI-bVII-I lift into major for the triumphant moment.
- Brass stabs on off-beats and accents (>) mark the hits; silence for a beat before a big entry.

## arranger
- Low end: cellos and basses in octaves, the synth bass on the root under them, low brass on the long notes.
- Brass stabs in fifths and octaves, not full triads, in the middle register. Choir on "ah" pads in the peak only.
- Build in layers: every 4-8 bars something enters (hi-hat-like shakers, a counter-ostinato, the choir), and a
  breakdown drops to ostinato and percussion before the last build.
- Humanise the orchestra lightly (timing_ms=5-10); keep the synths and the ostinato tight.

## rhythm
- Taiko and toms in a pattern that accents the ostinato (on 1, the "and" of 2, 4), big hits on section downbeats,
  a roll or flam into each change. Low hits mark the hits on picture.
- A trailer kick or boom on the strongest downbeats only; braams and impacts as events, not a beat.
- Bass: the synth bass doubles the ostinato's root in 8ths; side-chain or duck it under the taiko.

## sound
- Orchestral patches (ensemble strings, horns, trombones, choir) plus [reese_bass] or [analog_bass], [brass_synth]
  or a pulse synth for the hybrid layer; a taiko or cinematic percussion patch, not a pop kit.
- Short articulations need their own patch: the ostinato on spiccato or staccato strings.

## mix
- Big but clear: the low end shared out (kick and taiko under 80 Hz, synth bass 60-150, cellos and low brass
  above), high-pass everything else. SSL-style EQ and bus compression on the drums; Nova on the orchestra.
- One large hall on a bus for the orchestra and choir; the drums in a shorter room; the synths drier.
- Parallel or gentle bus compression on the percussion for weight; the orchestra uncompressed. Master -14 to
  -16 LUFS.

## review
- Does it drive? An ostinato that stops, a section with no new layer, or a peak no bigger than the verse is a
  fault.
- Mud is the usual failure: low brass, cellos, synth bass and taiko all in one band. Name the pair.
- The theme should survive the noise: audible in the peak against the drums.
