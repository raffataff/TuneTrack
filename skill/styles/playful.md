## producer
- 100-130 bpm, major with chromatic passing notes; a loop (30-60 s unless the brief asks for longer) that stays out of the player's way
  while they think.
- Pizzicato strings, mallets, clarinet or bassoon for humour, a plucky bass, light percussion; small and clear.

## composer
- A bouncy tune with staccato (') notes, grace notes and chromatic neighbours (a raised 4 to 5, a lowered 6 back
  to 5); question-answer phrases of two bars.
- Harmony: I-vi-ii-V, I-IV-V with a chromatic passing chord (#iv dim7, bVI for a surprise).
- Rests are jokes: a stop for a beat before the punchline note.

## arranger
- Pizzicato on the chords, mallets doubling the tune, bassoon or tuba on a staccato bass (oom-pah on 1 and 3,
  chord on 2 and 4).
- Trade the tune between voices every phrase; keep the texture thin enough to hear each one.
- Light humanising (timing_ms=5-10) on acoustic parts; tight on mallets.

## rhythm
- Woodblock, shaker, triangle, finger snaps, a light kick on 1; no heavy kit.
- Bass: staccato, bouncy, roots and fifths with chromatic approach notes into changes.

## sound
- Pizzicato strings, glockenspiel, marimba, xylophone, clarinet, bassoon, tuba, toy piano: patches or SFZ first;
  [mallet] and [pluck] otherwise.

## mix
- Bright and clear; a small room reverb. Every voice placed in its own spot left to right.
- Short sounds need little EQ; a high-pass on everything but the bass. Master -16 to -18 LUFS.

## review
- Heavy, dark or dense passages are out of style.
- Staccato that is not short enough, or a tune that does not bounce, misses the brief.
- The loop must not nag: flag a high note or accent that repeats too often.
